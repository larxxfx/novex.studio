In 1901, sponge divers raised a corroded lump of bronze from a Roman-era shipwreck off the Greek island of Antikythera. Inside were gears: at least 30 survive, cut by hand more than 2,000 years ago.

The Antikythera Mechanism tracked the Sun, the Moon and the planets, predicted eclipses and even counted down to the Olympic Games. Only about a third of it survives, in 82 fragments. In 2021 a UCL team led by Tony Freeth published a reconstruction of its front display, built from X-ray CT data.
