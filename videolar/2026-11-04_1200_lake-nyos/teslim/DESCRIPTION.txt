On the night of 21 August 1986, Lake Nyos in north-west Cameroon released a huge cloud of carbon dioxide. Heavier than air, the gas flowed down the valleys into sleeping villages. By morning 1,746 people and about 3,500 livestock were dead.

Scientists call it a limnic eruption: volcanic CO2 builds up in deep water until the lake can no longer hold it. Since 2001, degassing pipes have vented the gas safely. Two years earlier, nearby Lake Monoun had released gas too.
