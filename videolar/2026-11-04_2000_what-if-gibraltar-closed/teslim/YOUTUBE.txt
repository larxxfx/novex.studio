TITLE: What If the Strait of Gibraltar Closed?

DESCRIPTION:
A thought experiment, not a prediction: what if the Strait of Gibraltar closed?

The Mediterranean loses more water to evaporation than its rivers bring in; the Atlantic makes up the difference through a gap only about 13 km wide. Cut it off and the sea level would drop by roughly half a metre to a metre a year, leaving a salt desert kilometres below sea level within about a thousand years.

It has happened before: in the Messinian Salinity Crisis (5.96–5.33 million years ago) the Mediterranean largely dried out, until the Zanclean flood refilled most of it in as little as two years.
